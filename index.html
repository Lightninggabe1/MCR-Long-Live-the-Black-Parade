02

The Draag Storyline

Act I isn't a concert with visuals bolted on — it's a piece of theatre with a plot that has been advancing show by show since July 2025, with a genuine mid-tour twist. Here's the whole thing in order.

The Premise

Draag is a fictional authoritarian country ruled by the Grand Immortal Dictator. The band perform not as themselves but as The Black Parade — their 2007 marching-band alter ego — whose official title in-universe is "His Grand Immortal Dictator's National Band."

They are not performing because they want to. They're performing because they've been ordered to, to celebrate the regime.

The World Detail

  • Government ministries appear throughout: Menial Tasks, Complimentary Reconditioning, Operatic Relations
  • Draag has its own invented written language, Keposhka — 14 custom fonts, used down to the washing instructions on the merch
  • Its national anthem is "Over Fields"
  • Mock "rules" appear on screen pre-show, and they change city to city
2006 — Origin

The Album

The Black Parade is released: a concept record about a dying man, "the Patient," who meets death in the form of his fondest memory — a marching band. That marching band becomes the band's alter ego.

07 Oct 2007 — Mexico City

The Parade Dies

At the Palacio de los Deportes, the band kill off the alter ego onstage. "The Black Parade is dead!" That performance was released as a live album. For seventeen years, that was the end of it.

Jan–Feb 2026 — Latin America (chronologically first)

Inside the M.O.A.T.

This leg is set entirely inside the M.O.A.T. — a prison-hospital where the Black Parade have been held since 2007 for "reconditioning." The band appear as patients: nurses and orderlies hand them medication mid-song. Their uniforms are the original 2007 ones, not the newer versions.

Key inversion: The Clerk, an antagonist in the 2025 shows, is here a fellow inmate who shakes hands with the band. And at the end, Gerard's character kills him — the reverse of what happens later.

14 Feb 2026 — Mexico City

The Twist Lands

On the final Latin American night, Gerard is handed the 2025 jacket onstage — confirming that everything in this leg was a prequel to the shows that had already happened. The audience had been watching the story out of order for a month without knowing.

The reveal was partly an accident of logistics: production designer Rob Sinclair proposed the prequel framing because the full-scale production physically couldn't travel to the region.

Jul 2025 – Sep 2025 — North America

Released to Perform

Chronologically the second act. The Parade's "work privilege" is reinstated and they tour for the regime. The Clerk is now the villain, relaying the Dictator's orders. Gerard's character is punished for refusing to comply.

"Welcome to the Black Parade" is reframed as a nationalist address delivered from a podium over propaganda footage. Afterwards the crowd votes in a mock election — using yea/nay cards handed out at the gate — on whether to execute four people. It's rigged. The execution happens regardless. (In Tampa the "nay" option was simply removed from the cards.)

Late Act I — Every Night

Rebellion, Then Collapse

During "Mama," Gerard steals a dagger from one of the Dictator's servants, calling it a tool for our treasonous needs. "Sleep" is staged over a simulated nuclear launch. "Teenagers" runs against fake Draag consumer adverts. "Famous Last Words" sets the stage on fire.

Then the reprise of "The End." — the Clerk returns dressed as a Pierrot, stabs Gerard in the throat, and the rest of the band are dragged off by soldiers. The Pierrot dances to "Blood," reveals a bomb vest, and detonates it. Blackout. End of Act I.

Aug–Oct 2026 — Current Leg

Where The Story Sits Now

The tour is mid-run through North America as you read this, wrapping with five nights at the Hollywood Bowl through Halloween. The M.O.A.T. institutional staging has carried forward, more pre-recorded propaganda speeches have been added, and the full B-stage is back in play.

19–20 Nov 2026 — You Are Here

Kuala Lumpur

Your nights are the second-to-last stop of the entire tour — only Jakarta follows, on the 22nd/23rd, closing out a run of roughly 47 shows across seven legs and two years. Whatever the story's ending is, KL is where it very nearly lands.

What this means for you Two nights, and the show is deliberately different night to night — different pre-show rules, different Act II, occasional plot movement. If you're going to both, treat them as two chapters rather than a repeat. If you're going to one, night two historically carries slightly more weight on multi-night stands, since that's where they tend to place the bigger swings.
03

How the B-Stage Works

The single most misunderstood part of this show — and the thing that should decide where you stand.

The Mechanic

Act I ends with the Pierrot's bomb. Blackout. Then a cellist — Clarice Jensen — plays a solo interlude while the band physically walk out into the middle of the crowd to a second, smaller stage.

They come back out of costume, as My Chemical Romance rather than The Black Parade. No theatrics, no characters, no plot. Roughly 10–12 songs of a straight rock show.

Why It Matters

Multiple critics who reviewed the 2025 run called the B-stage the better half of the night — the Act I spectacle is astonishing, but the B-stage is where the surprises, the deep cuts and the raw energy live.

It also completely reorganises the geography of the venue. Seats that were mediocre for Act I can become the best in the house, and vice versa.

Will KL Have One?

Genuinely uncertain — plan for both. The B-stage was dropped for the entire Latin American leg purely because the production couldn't be freighted at full scale. It returned for Europe and is running now in North America.

Bukit Jalil is a large open stadium, which is the right shape for it. But Southeast Asia is a fly-in leg with the same logistics problem Latin America had, and the arena shows in Bangkok and Singapore almost certainly can't host one.

If it's dropped, Act II simply happens on the main stage — you lose nothing musically.

What You'll Be Looking At

The production is designed by Rob Sinclair. Based on how the tour has run through 2026, here's what to expect at Bukit Jalil — with the caveat that this is a projection from the current leg, not a confirmed KL plot.

The Institution

The 2026 staging is set inside the M.O.A.T. — read it as a state psychiatric facility rather than a concert stage. Clinical, institutional, deliberately drained of colour.

Background performers dressed as nurses and orderlies move through the set and hand the band medication mid-performance. It's unsettling on purpose.

The Uniforms

For 2026 the band reverted to costumes closely resembling the original 2007 Black Parade uniforms — a deliberate signal that this is chronologically earlier in the story than the 2025 shows.

Watch for Gerard removing the jacket. It's a plot beat, not a temperature decision.

The Screens

The video content isn't a live feed with graphics — it's a running stream of in-universe Draag propaganda: state broadcasts, wanted posters, fake consumer adverts during "Teenagers," and pre-show "rules" that change city to city.

The 2026 leg added more pre-recorded speeches than 2025. Expect a lot of the Dictator's face.

Pyro & Effects

Heavy. "Famous Last Words" is staged with the set apparently going up in flames. "Sleep" runs over a simulated nuclear launch sequence. Expect real heat if you're near the front.

Confetti at the climax. Bring nothing you'd mind getting covered.

The Extra Musicians

This is a much bigger ensemble than four people. Alongside the band: Jarrod Alexander (drums), Jamie Muhoberac (keys), Tucker Rule (percussion), Kayleigh Goldsworthy (violin, on "Cancer" and "Mama") and Clarice Jensen (cello, interlude).

The Cast

  • The Grand Immortal Dictator — Wayne Jay. Watches from a distance, mostly on screen.
  • The Clerk — Charlie Saxton. Also becomes the Pierrot.
  • Marianne — Lucy Joy Altus, opera singer.
  • Sylvia — Charlotte Kelso, alternates in as "head nurse."
  • The Gentleman — a ventriloquist dummy the band met in prison.

Running Order & Night-Of Notes

Approximate shape of the evening, based on how the tour has run. Doors and set times for KL will be confirmed by Hello Universe closer to the date — check before you travel.

Pre-Show

Rules & Voting Cards

Mock Draag "rules" go up on the screens. At some shows attendees have been handed voting cards on entry — take one, you'll need it. The PA plays a 1956 recording of "Tonight You Belong to Me" by Patience and Prudence before things start.

Support

Opening Act

The tour has used a different opener in nearly every city. KL's has not been widely confirmed — check the promoter's channels.

Act I Opens

"Over Fields"

An opera singer performs the Draag national anthem and asks the crowd to join in. Then the band walk out in uniform and go straight into "The End."

Act I Midpoint

The Election

"Welcome to the Black Parade" is delivered as a political speech from a podium. Immediately after, the mock vote on four executions. Hold your card up. The result is predetermined.

Act I Close

The Pierrot

Gerard is stabbed during the reprise of "The End." The band are removed by soldiers. The Pierrot detonates a bomb vest to "Blood." Then darkness — don't leave, this is the interval.

Interlude

Cello Solo

Clarice Jensen plays while the band cross to the B-stage. This is your window for water or the toilets if you need it — but you'll miss the walk-out.

Act II

The Rock Show

Costumes off. Roughly 10–12 songs, no narrative, ending on "Helena" or close to it. This is the loudest the stadium will get all night.

Practical, Bukit Jalil-Specific

  • Open-air stadium in November — Klang Valley evening thunderstorms are routine. Ticketing has been rain-or-shine elsewhere on this tour. Bring a thin poncho, not an umbrella.
  • LRT Sri Petaling line to Bukit Jalil is the sane route in; the crush getting out afterwards is significant. Budget an hour.
  • 60,000+ per night. Agree a meeting point with anyone you're going with before you go in.
  • Heavy pyro plus tropical humidity — hydrate through the day, not just at the venue.

Worth Doing Beforehand

  • Listen to The Black Parade straight through, in order, at least twice. Act I is exactly this, and sequence familiarity matters more than lyric perfection.
  • Learn "Over Fields."
  • Watch the original tour announcement trailer for the Draag world setup.
  • Check setlist.fm the morning after Bangkok (17 Nov) and Manila (14 Nov) — those will tell you exactly what this leg's Act II looks like, two days before your show.
