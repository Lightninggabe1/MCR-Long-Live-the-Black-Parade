01

Two Shows, One Tour

The single most important thing to understand, and the thing almost nobody tells you: MCR are touring two different productions simultaneously. Some cities get the MOAT show. Others get the DRAAG show. Same album, same running order, radically different staging, imagery and meaning — and the band don't announce which one you're getting.

The MOAT Show

Interior · Prison-Hospital · You Are An Inmate
  • MOAT is widely read by fans as Ministry of Approved Therapy — a reconditioning camp where the state brainwashes prisoners into compliance.
  • Prison and hospital imagery throughout. The jumbotron shows the stage framed from behind prison bars.
  • Each band member takes medication at the top of the show, handed over by orderlies.
  • The band wear tattered versions of the original 2007 uniforms. Other characters wear prison fatigues with ID numbers.
  • You, the audience, are positioned as fellow prisoners — a test audience watching the band rehearse.
  • Also nicknamed the "Phantom" show after the Phantom-of-the-Opera staging it leans on.
  • No mock election. This is the big practical difference.

The DRAAG Show

Exterior · Military Parade · You Are A Citizen
  • Set in the country of Draag itself. You are a citizen or a tourist, attending a state spectacle.
  • Militaristic imagery — parade staging, propaganda broadcasts, nationalist pageantry.
  • Contains the "Practice of Democracy" segment: the mock election and public execution.
  • You're handed a card on entry — red side "Yea", black side "Nea", printed in both English and Keposhkan.
  • Four hooded figures are brought out. Gerard reads their crimes, usually something trivial. The crowd votes.
  • The vote never matters. They're executed by firing squad regardless, to cheering.
Which will Kuala Lumpur get? Unknown, and not announced in advance. Two things are worth holding in mind. First, you have two nights — on multi-night stands the band have varied the production, so there's a real chance of seeing both. Second, the DRAAG show's election segment needs cards physically distributed to 60,000 people, which is a promoter-side logistics job; if you're handed a red-and-black card at the gate, you're at a DRAAG show. If the screens show prison bars and the band swallow pills in the opening minutes, you're in the MOAT.
Why the split exists at all It isn't just a staging variation — it's a storytelling device. The MOAT show is chronologically earlier: the band are imprisoned and being reconditioned. The DRAAG show is what they were reconditioned for: performing for the regime in public. Seeing one makes the other land harder. Seeing both, in order, is the complete text.
02

The Storyline, Up To Now

This has been running since July 2025 and has moved considerably. It includes a genuine mid-tour reveal, a coup, and — as of September — a change of government.

2006 — The Source Text

The Patient

The Black Parade is a concept album about a dying man — "the Patient" — who meets death in the form of his fondest childhood memory: a marching band. That marching band became the band's stage alter ego.

07 Oct 2007 — Mexico City

The Parade Dies

The band kill off the alter ego onstage. "The Black Parade is dead!" Seventeen years of silence follow. In-universe, this is the moment the Parade is seized and sent to the MOAT.

Chronologically First — The MOAT

Reconditioning

The Black Parade are held in the MOAT and systematically broken. During "Sleep", the screens show the other characters undergoing the same reconditioning — the Clerk, the Secretary, and even the Grand Immortal Dictator himself. Only Marianne and Sylvia are never shown being processed.

A fellow prisoner, the Gentleman, teaches them the "Dagger" interlude — a tongue-in-cheek protest song smuggled into "Mama." The plan: play the puppet, then turn.

Jul 2025 – Sep 2025 — First Public Tour

His Grand Immortal Dictator's National Band

The Parade's "work privilege" is reinstated. They perform to boost the Dictator's image and distract the population. "Welcome to the Black Parade" is reframed as a nationalist address from a podium. The Clerk is the villain, relaying orders. Gerard is punished for non-compliance, and ends each night murdered onstage.

14 Feb 2026 — Mexico City

The Prequel Reveal

On the final Latin American night, Gerard is handed the 2025 jacket onstage — confirming the entire leg had been a prequel. Audiences had watched a month of shows out of chronological order without being told.

Production designer Rob Sinclair proposed the framing after concluding the full-scale show couldn't be freighted to the region. A logistics constraint became the tour's best twist.

Aug 2026 — Citi Field onward

Going Off Script

The surveillance eye above the stage — which has watched, judged and broadcast propaganda all night — begins to malfunction during "Famous Last Words." It darts frantically, throws sparks, and lowers to ground level as the band play an unscheduled reprise of "Welcome to the Black Parade." The regime's monitoring system does not know what to do with a band that has stopped following orders.

12 Sep 2026 — San Antonio

The Coup

The most significant plot development of the entire tour. The mock election is turned on its owner: the vote comes back "YEA" to execute the Grand Immortal Dictator — and to install Gerard in his place. Gerard beheads him, then assumes power through a second mock election.

At Louisville, the kill was staged to "Vesti la giubba" — the clown's aria from Pagliacci, about a performer forced to perform while his life collapses. At another show it was Ray Toro who became Dictator instead.

Now — October 2026

A New Dictator, And A Hint

Gerard's character now rules Draag. During the B-Show after taking power, he hinted at a new album — the first serious signal of one since "The Foundations of Decay" in 2022.

The tour has five Hollywood Bowl nights through Halloween, then flies to Asia. Where the story goes between now and November is genuinely unwritten.

19–20 Nov 2026 — You Are Here

Kuala Lumpur

Your two nights are the penultimate stop of the whole enterprise — only Jakarta follows on the 22nd/23rd. After roughly two years and seven legs, the Black Parade is going to die again, for the last time, within days of your show.

The theory that makes it all cohere The dominant fan reading is that the tour is a time loop across a multiverse. The band are doomed to repeat this performance, and this death, in reality after reality — and they are becoming aware of it. Before "Sleep," Gerard and the Clerk draw chalk outlines on the stage floor and lie down inside them together, chatting, staring up. As the tour has gone on, the Man on Fire has stopped running — he now simply walks across the stage, fully alight, entirely desensitised.
03

Who's On Stage

Half the show's meaning is carried by characters who never sing a note. Knowing them in advance is the difference between watching spectacle and watching a story.

The Grand Immortal Dictator

Wayne Jay

Ruler of Draag. Mostly watches from screens and from a distance. The regime is modelled on a composite of Soviet brutalism and the militaristic pageantry of fascist Europe.

Status: deposed. As of San Antonio he has been executed onstage and replaced by Gerard — though a dictatorship that recycles its leader through a rigged vote is rather the point.

The Clerk / The Clown

Charlie Saxton

The tour's most complex figure. A put-upon middle functionary who carries tea trays and hands the band props. Disrespected by his employers, thanklessly overworked — he's the working class under the dictatorship, played for comedy and for sympathy at once.

At some shows he returns during "The End." dressed as a Pierrot clown, slits Gerard's throat, reveals a suicide vest and detonates the stage. At others he is the victim instead. He has also sung the Draag anthem live.

The Secretary — "Veronika"

Gemma Scoble

The Dictator's right hand. Suit, sunglasses, Doberman. She supervises the band throughout, making sure they don't deviate, and her presence alone commands the stage.

She has occasionally taken the Clown role herself and killed the Clerk. At one show she used the Gentleman puppet to break up a fight between Gerard and the Clerk.

The Gentleman

Brandon Joel Rothstein

Exists as both puppet and human. Paraded around as a marionette during "Mama"; appears on screen in human form during "Teenagers," where he hosts Draag's children's programming — twisted nostalgia as propaganda delivery.

At San Antonio a pamphlet, "A Record of The Gentleman," was handed to the crowd. It implies he was the regime's first puppet, hosted a beloved propaganda children's show, was returned to the MOAT, and there escaped their control — becoming the one who taught the band the Dagger interlude.

Marianne & Sylvia

Lucy Joy Altus · Charlotte Kelso

The opera singers who perform the Draag anthem and the guest part in "Mama." Marianne wore sunglasses and walked with a cane. Sylvia replaced her wearing an eyepatch, with no cane and no explanation. Marianne was simply erased; Sylvia behaves as though she was always there.

They are the only characters never shown being reconditioned — not even the Dictator can say that. The leading theory: the eye above the stage is Sylvia's missing one, and she, not the Dictator, is running the loop.

The Man on Fire

Uncredited

A one-off. During "Mama" a man runs across the stage fully engulfed in flames. Nobody onstage reacts. He is never seen again and the production has never named the performer.

He reads as normalised atrocity — horror that the regime has made unremarkable. His gradual shift from running to walking over the course of the tour is one of the clearest signals of the time-loop reading.

The Second Clerk

—

A supervisor over the Clerk, one rung up the bureaucracy and equally unrecognised. He takes it out on the only person with less power than him. A small, precise piece of writing about how hierarchies reproduce cruelty downward.

The Musicians

Beyond the four
  • Jarrod Alexander — drums
  • Jamie Muhoberac — keys
  • Tucker Rule — percussion
  • Kayleigh Goldsworthy — violin, on "Cancer" and "Mama"
  • Clarice Jensen — cello; plays the solo interlude between the two shows

DRAAG shows only · Position varies

05

The Endings Log

"Blood" has multiple alternate endings and they have become progressively more unhinged as the tour has gone on. This is what's been documented on the current leg — and it's the single best reason to go both nights.

Citi FieldNew York · 09 Aug

The eye plays out the Clerk's memories while he is wheeled out on a stretcher. Gerard stabs him to death — blood spraying across the stage — and pulls out his organs. Staged as "medical treatment."

Comerica ParkDetroit · 21 Aug

A Phantom/MOAT show. Gerard slits Frank Iero's throat — the violence turning inward, onto the band itself.

Target FieldMinneapolis · 24 Aug

The band perform a goofy kick-line dance number to "Blood," confetti falling — and then the Clerk cuts all of their throats. Comedy and slaughter in the same ninety seconds.

AlamodomeSan Antonio · 12 Sep

The structural turning point. Gerard beheads the Grand Immortal Dictator and takes power through a second rigged election. He hints at a new album during the B-Show that follows.

Louder Than LifeLouisville · 18 Sep

Gerard kills the Dictator again, this time staged to "Vesti la giubba" — the aria of the clown who must perform while his life falls apart.

Recurring VariantsAcross the leg

The classic: the Clerk returns as the Pierrot, slits Gerard's throat, reveals a suicide vest and bombs the stage. Occasionally the Secretary takes the Clown role and kills the Clerk instead. Roles reverse without explanation.

What this means for 19 and 20 November Nobody can tell you what your ending will be — that's the design. But two consecutive nights at the same venue, this late in the tour, with the story having already reached a change of government, is as good a position as any fan on this tour has had. Do not leave after "Famous Last Words." The ending is the point.
06

Symbols To Watch For

Four recurring images carry most of the subtext. Spot these and the show reads completely differently.

The Eye

An enormous eye looms above the band all night. It watches, it judges, it displays messages and relays propaganda. It is the regime's surveillance made physical.

Its breakdown during "Famous Last Words" is the clearest plot beat in the show. The theory worth holding: it is Sylvia's missing eye, the one behind the patch.

Wheat

Projected constantly and written into the anthem. It reads two ways at once — the Bolshevik peace, land and bread, serfs farming land they'll never own; and "bread and circuses," the state pacifying its population with food and entertainment.

The band are the circus. You bought a ticket to it. That's the joke, and you're in it.

Keposhka

Draag's invented written language, commissioned by Gerard Way from typographer Nate Piekos. Fourteen custom fonts. More symbols than letters, with certain letter combinations fusing into unique glyphs.

Used everywhere, including the washing instructions on the merch. Fans have built translators and a Keposhkan Wordle.

Chalk Outlines

Before "Sleep," Gerard and the Clerk draw crime-scene body outlines on the stage and lie down inside their own. They talk. They look up at the sky.

Two characters who have worked out they're going to die again tonight, and the night after, taking a moment together before it happens.

08

The B-Stage

Fans and the band call the two halves the A-Show and the B-Show. They're almost different events.

The Mechanic

Act I ends in blackout. Cellist Clarice Jensen plays a solo — "Metastable," "From A to B" or "Romance" — while the band physically walk out into the middle of the crowd to a second stage.

They return out of costume, as My Chemical Romance. No characters, no plot, no Draag. Ten to twelve songs of a straight rock show.

Why It Matters

Multiple critics called the B-Show the better half. It's where the rarities land, where the band talk candidly — and, on this leg, where Gerard hinted at a new album after taking power in the A-Show.

It also reorganises the venue's geography entirely. Mediocre Act I seats can become the best in the house.

Will KL Have One?

Genuinely uncertain. The B-stage was dropped for all of Latin America on freight grounds and the second set moved to the main stage. It returned for Europe and is running now in North America.

Bukit Jalil is a large open stadium — the right shape for it. But Southeast Asia is a fly-in leg with the same constraint Latin America had, and the arena shows in Bangkok and Singapore almost certainly can't host one. If it's dropped you lose nothing musically.

Running Order & Night-Of

Approximate shape of the evening. Doors and set times for KL will be confirmed by Hello Universe closer to the date.

Pre-Show

Read The Room

Mock Draag rules on the screens. Were you handed a red-and-black card? That's a DRAAG show and there will be an election. Prison bars on the jumbotron and pills onstage means MOAT. A period record plays over the PA.

Support

Opening Act

Different in nearly every city. KL's hasn't been widely confirmed — check the promoter.

A-Show Opens

"Over Fields"

The Draag anthem, sung live over tape, crowd invited in. Wheat on the screens. Then straight into "The End."

A-Show Mid

The Election

DRAAG shows only. Hold your card up. The result is predetermined — though on recent nights the target has been the Dictator himself.

A-Show Turn

The Eye Breaks

During "Famous Last Words" the stage burns and the surveillance eye malfunctions. This is the moment the band go off script. Watch the eye, not the pyro.

A-Show Close

"Blood"

Gerard and the Clerk, alone. Do not leave. This is the part that's different every night and it's where the story actually resolves.

Interlude

Cello Solo

Clarice Jensen plays while the band cross over. Your window for water — but you'll miss the walk-out.

B-Show

The Rock Show

Costumes off, characters gone. Ten to twelve songs, no narrative, ending on or near "Helena." Loudest the stadium gets all night — and where any new-album hints have landed.

Practical · Bukit Jalil

  • Open-air stadium in November — Klang Valley evening storms are routine. Thin poncho, not an umbrella.
  • LRT Sri Petaling line is the sane route in. Budget an hour to get out afterwards.
  • 60,000+ a night. Agree a meeting point before you go in.
  • Heavy pyro plus tropical humidity — hydrate across the day, not just at the venue.
  • Keep the card if you're given one. People have kept them as the best piece of merch they didn't pay for.

Worth Doing Beforehand

  • Listen to The Black Parade straight through twice. Sequence familiarity matters more than lyric perfection.
  • Learn "Over Fields."
  • Watch the 2024 tour announcement trailer for the Draag setup, and the "Phantom" teaser.
  • Check setlist.fm after Manila (14 Nov) and Bangkok (17 Nov) — two days before your show, those will tell you this leg's exact Act II and which variant is touring.
  • Follow the Hollywood Bowl Halloween shows (30–31 Oct). The story may move substantially there, and it's three weeks before you go.
